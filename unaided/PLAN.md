# Unaided

*Five minutes a day of doing it yourself, scored, so you can prove what you can still do without the machine.*

Unaided is a daily five-minute session of short tasks done with no assistant, no search and no calculator: hold a passage in memory and answer questions on it after ten minutes of other work, do arithmetic in your head, estimate a quantity, write a paragraph, follow a chain of reasoning, spell, keep your bearings. Every task is generated fresh and adapts to you. You get an Unaided score from 0 to 100, per skill and overall, a streak, and a record of how the score moves over months. Later, a verified session becomes a credential an employer or a school can trust, because it was done under conditions no AI could reach.

The bet: by 2031, "what can you do unaided" becomes a question employers, schools and licensing bodies ask explicitly, because AI has made the assisted version of every task worthless as evidence. The product that turns that question into a daily habit gets the same position Duolingo holds for languages, on a fear that is larger and more universal than wanting to learn Spanish.

## Why this, and why now

**The atrophy is measured, not speculative.** MIT Media Lab's EEG study (54 participants, 2025) found people writing with an LLM showed the weakest neural connectivity of three groups and could quote only about 17% of their own essay minutes later, against about 46% for people who wrote unaided. The authors call the effect cognitive debt. One finding is the design brief for this product: participants who wrote unaided first and used the model afterwards kept both the recall and the brain engagement; those who started with the model and then had to work alone did not. Order matters. Unaided first. [arxiv 2506.08872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872), [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/your-brain-on-chatgpt/overview/)

**Confidence in AI displaces thinking.** Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon (CHI 2025) surveyed 319 knowledge workers across 936 real tasks: the more they trusted the AI on a task, the less critical thinking they reported doing, and workers who lacked the skill to check the output stopped checking. [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)

**A regulator already mandated the fix, in the one industry that automated first.** After Air France 447, the FAA issued SAFO 13002 (2013) and SAFO 17007 (2017), telling airlines that continuous use of automation erodes manual flying skill and that pilots should hand-fly on purpose during low-workload phases. Airline training programmes were required to add manual slow flight, upset recovery and stall recovery. The aviation system did not ban autopilot. It mandated practice without it. That is the template for every profession now automating its judgement. [FAA SAFO 13002](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/other_visit/aviation_industry/airline_operators/airline_safety/SAFO13002.pdf), [FAA SAFO 17007](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/SAFO17007.pdf)

**Institutions are already retreating to unaided assessment, by hand.** Texas A&M, the University of Florida and UC Berkeley report surging blue-book demand; Middlebury has moved some courses to oral exams; the Washington Post's editorial line in August 2026 is that universities' fight against AI cheating "doesn't go far enough". In hiring, 38.5% of 19,368 technical interviews between July 2025 and January 2026 showed signs of AI cheating (48% in purely technical roles); Interview Coder claims 150,000 users and Final Round AI claims 10 million. Employers' response is live problem-solving with commentary, pair sessions and proctored, identity-verified credentials, whose recognition in job postings grew 31% year on year. Every one of those is a costly, ad hoc way of asking the same question Unaided answers cheaply and continuously. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/14/ai-blue-books-colleges-jobs), [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-08-oral-exams-comeback-ai-problems.html), [We Recruit IT](https://werecruit.it/blog/ai-cheating-interviews-2026/), [noon.ai](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/290-top-10-skills-assessment-tools)

**The apprenticeship pipeline is breaking, so the credential has to change.** Wharton's Ethan Mollick argues the white-collar apprenticeship model has broken: juniors use AI instead of doing the work, and managers assign the work to AI instead of to juniors. Stanford's Brynjolfsson finds fewer juniors hired in software. When nobody can tell from output whether a person can do the work, evidence of unaided ability becomes the scarce signal. [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/this-week-in-business/where-artificial-intelligence-stands-heading-into-2026/)

**People already feel it, and say so.** Among 2,500 US adults aged 18 to 28 (October 2025), 79% said AI makes people lazier and 62% said it makes them less smart. 46% of Gen Z workers say AI is making them dumber. 66% of US workers worry AI is eroding their writing; the same share, their creativity. More than half of US adults worry AI will erode human skills. This is the largest self-reported anxiety about AI that no product addresses directly. [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2026/06/ai-concerns-americans-adults-human-skills), [Fortune / Wharton](https://fortune.com/2026/01/30/gen-z-believes-ai-making-colleagues-dumb-and-lazy-critical-thinking-wharton-led-survey/), [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/policy-not-pr-will-determine-gen-zs-trust-in-ai/)

## Why it becomes a habit

- **The loop is proven.** A short daily set, a score, a streak, a shareable result. Duolingo has 58.7M daily users and 10M with year-long streaks; a learner with a 7-day streak is 2.4x more likely to return tomorrow. NYT Games earns a daily check-in from millions with a two-minute puzzle. Unaided is a two-to-five-minute set that changes every day and cannot be finished faster with AI, which is the one thing Duolingo cannot say. [pulse2](https://pulse2.com/duolingo-daily-active-users-reach-58-7-million-as-retention-hits-record-84-and-social-accounts-top-1-billion-organic-impressions/), [Forbes on NYT Games](https://www.forbes.com/sites/erikkain/2026/09/02/nyt-games-bonus-puzzles-wordle-in-1-connections-3x3/)
- **The fear is about identity, and it compounds.** "Am I getting dumber?" is a question people ask themselves more each year as they delegate more. A number that answers it, and that they can move, is a reason to come back that grows with AI adoption rather than fading.
- **Loss aversion twice over.** The streak, and the score itself. Skipping a week does not just break a streak; the drills get easier and the score drifts down, visibly.
- **A number worth showing.** "Unaided 74" is a brag that AI cannot fake, which makes it more shareable than any AI-assisted output.

## Confidence, decomposed

The brief asked for better than 80% confidence that the product is both necessary and habit-forming within five years.

**Necessary by 2031 (employers, schools or licensing bodies routinely require demonstrated unaided competence, and people seek to maintain it):**

| Step | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| AI assistance stays on the trajectory that makes assisted output useless as evidence of ability | 0.95 | Business AI adoption 7.5% (Jan 2023) to 55% (Jun 2026) per Ramp; 38.5% of technical interviews already show AI cheating |
| Given that, institutions respond by testing unaided ability rather than banning AI | 0.90 | Already happening: blue books, oral exams, proctored credentials up 31%; the FAA precedent shows the mature form |
| Given both, individuals adopt deliberate unaided practice at scale rather than only taking one-off tests | 0.85 | 79% of young adults already believe AI makes people lazier; the same demand that built an $8B brain-training market on far weaker evidence |
| Product | **0.73** | |
| Correlation adjustment: these three move together (each makes the next more likely) | **+0.07** | |

Estimate for the category being necessary: **about 0.80**.

**Habit-forming (a product in this category reaches Duolingo-class retention):** **0.65 to 0.75.** Higher than any product I would pitch on a novel mechanic, because the mechanic is borrowed intact from products with 58M daily users and the content cannot be shortcut with AI. Lower than certain because a drill is work and the first week has to be won by design (short sets, immediate marks, a baseline the person wants to beat).

**This implementation wins the category:** **0.15 to 0.25.** Higher than usual for a specific product because the incumbents are structurally unable to follow and the indie signal (Atrophy) shows nobody serious has moved.

**What would change my mind.** A large study showing no transfer between unaided practice and unaided performance in real tasks (I put this under 15%: the tasks here are the real tasks, not proxies). Employers settling on "AI-assisted only" hiring with no unaided component anywhere (under 10%; the direction of travel is the opposite). A vendor shipping a credible "unaided mode" credential inside ChatGPT or Claude (under 20%; it contradicts their retention interest, and the credential must be vendor-neutral to be trusted).

**Pre-mortem.** If it fails: (1) the sessions felt like school, so the copy, the pace and the marks must feel like a sport, not a test; (2) the score did not move, so early sessions must be tuned so a person gains ten points in the first two weeks from practice alone; (3) claims drifted toward health ("prevents decline"), which is the Lumosity trap; the product claims only the measured score, always.

## The habit loop, specified

1. **Trigger.** One notification a day, at the time the person first opened the app. Nothing else. Skipping a day ends the streak; the app says so once, not repeatedly.
2. **Session.** Seven tasks, about five minutes: read a passage once; six arithmetic items; three estimates; four reasoning items; three bearing chains; six spellings; one paragraph of at least 40 words with paste and autocorrect off; then three questions on the passage read ten minutes earlier. Difficulty per skill adapts (five levels): 85% or better moves a level up, under 40% moves down.
3. **Mark.** Every item marked immediately with the right answer. The paragraph is marked by Claude (clarity, substance, mechanics, one sentence of feedback) when the person allows it, otherwise by the person against the same rubric.
4. **Score.** Each skill moves a third of the way from its old value to today's result. The overall score is the mean. The first session is the baseline, stamped as such.
5. **Return.** A results card that names the weakest skill and promises tomorrow's set leans on it. A share card that AI cannot fake.
6. **Long loop.** A history chart; monthly "unaided report"; and, when verified sessions ship, a credential.

## What exists now

`unaided/app.html` in this repository is a working single-file prototype of the full daily loop: all seven task types, generated fresh each session, adaptive difficulty, the delayed-recall test, paste-off writing with Claude marking when available and self-marking when not, per-skill and overall scores, streak, history, and a share card. It stores everything on the device. It has no dependencies and runs from a file. It was driven end to end in a headless browser before this document was written.

It is a prototype: no accounts, no verification, no notifications, no leagues. Those are the plan.

## The plan

**Days 1 to 90: prove the loop.**
1. Ship to 500 people who self-describe as heavy AI users; onboarding is the baseline session and nothing else.
2. Add the daily notification, a 14-day history chart, and the share card as an image.
3. Tune difficulty so median users gain 8 to 12 points in the first two weeks (practice effect), then plateau; the plateau is where honesty starts and churn is measured.
4. Kill criterion: D7 retention under 25% or median session under 2 minutes (people skipping the writing).

**Months 4 to 12: make it a sport and a signal.**
1. Friends and leagues by weekly score gain, not absolute score, so a beginner can win.
2. Assisted mode: once a week, the same set with an AI allowed, to show the person's own "AI gap" (assisted minus unaided). The gap is the most interesting number in the product and the one nobody else can compute.
3. Skill packs by profession: code (the Atrophy drill set, licensed or rebuilt), clinical reasoning, legal drafting, spreadsheet arithmetic. Each pack is a B2B door.
4. Verified session: camera on, identity-checked, randomised set, no other windows; produces a signed Unaided report. Sell to employers as a pre-interview screen at a fraction of a proctored assessment's price, and to universities as an oral-exam substitute.

**Years 2 to 5: the credential.**
1. Unaided score on the CV next to the degree; verified reports accepted by a first cohort of employers who are already running live-commentary interviews to solve the same problem.
2. Institutional licensing: a school or company runs Unaided for everyone, sees aggregate unaided ability over time, and can prove it to a regulator or an accreditor. This is the FAA SAFO, productised.
3. Public annual report: how unaided ability is moving across a large population by age and profession. That report is the moat, because nobody else will have the data.

## Business model

- Free: the daily session, the score, the streak, history.
- Plus ($5 a month): AI marking of writing on every session, the weekly assisted-mode gap, leagues, profession packs.
- Verified ($29 per report, or employer-paid): identity-checked sessions with a signed report.
- Institutions (per seat): dashboards, cohort reports, integration with hiring and learning systems.
- Never: selling individual data, and never any claim about brain health or disease.

## Risks

- **It feels like school.** Everything in the copy and pace is built against this: "Hand it in" not "Submit", marks not grades, a stamp that says baseline, weakest-skill honesty.
- **Health-claim drift.** The Lumosity settlement is the fence. The product says "what you can still do by yourself", never "prevents decline".
- **Cheating the drills with AI.** Possible in the free product and pointless: the score is private and the person only cheats themselves; the verified session is where it matters and is proctored.
- **Content quality.** Passages, estimates and prompts must be accurate and varied; the prototype ships 8 passages, 30 estimates, 30 spellings and 10 prompts, enough for a month; the product needs thousands, generated by a model and reviewed by people.
- **Name and domain.** "Unaided" is short, says exactly what the product is, and unaided.app, unaided.co, unaided.io, unaided.dev and getunaided.com were all available on 2026-09-04 (checked through the registrar). unaided.ai and unaided.com are taken. Register the .app before the test cohort.
